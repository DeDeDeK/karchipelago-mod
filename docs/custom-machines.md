# Custom Machines

`mods/custom_machines/` adds air ride machines - stars or bikes - that vanilla does not have. It is a registry in the `custom_*` family: it owns no machine of its own, it scans a drop-in folder on the disc and widens the engine tables so whatever it finds gets a real `MachineKind`, a real slot in its own machine class, audio rows and (optionally) a `CharacterKind` with a cell on the select grids. Every machine is authored whole in its own archive; the one thing a machine borrows from vanilla is the voice it uses where it ships no sound of its own.

## Where a machine comes from

Every `.dat` under the FST folder `machines/` is a candidate. An archive qualifies when it exports two publics: its own `vcData` symbol, holding an ordinary engine machine archive of its class exactly as `VcStarSlick.dat` or `VcWheelWheelie.dat` ship theirs, and `customMachine`, a `CustomMachineDesc` saying how to register it.

`CustomMachineDesc` is declared in `mods/custom_machines/src/custom_machine_desc.h`, which carries the field-by-field notes, alongside the art side-car's `CustomMachineArt`, the stat row enums and the magic, version, extension and folder defines. The on-disc formats are private to the mod - discovery is their only reader - so the public API header carries none of them. In outline: the display name, which is also the handle consumers find the machine by, and the `vcData` public to register; the machine class, star or bike; whether the machine also wants a `CharacterKind` and a select-grid cell, and the `RiderKind` for that row; the `MachineKind` of its own class whose sounds it uses where it ships none of its own (`audio_kind`); a City Trial loose-spawn weight; a select-screen blurb; up to four vehicle particle generators of its own; its CPU rows; the archive, its public and the vanilla legendary index for the assembly cutscene; the patch-stat scaling rows its class keeps per machine; and the two heights the engine keeps per kind for the field blip and the stat radar screen.

`spawn_weight` is weighed against a vanilla table whose per-machine weights run 6-10 out of a ~111-119 total, so 1.0 is roughly 0.8% of field spawns.

A descriptor whose `version` is not `CUSTOM_MACHINE_DESC_VERSION` is rejected at discovery and reported, never read partially.

Registration order is FST scan order, and it decides the appended kinds: the *n*-th machine found takes `MachineKind` `VCKIND_NUM + n` whatever its class, and the next free appended slot of its own class - star slot `19 + s` for the *s*-th star, bike slot `7 + b` for the *b*-th bike. `CharacterKind`s come off a separate counter that only advances for a machine that asked for one, so they run `CKIND_NUM` upward in the order of the machines with `wants_character`, not in registry order. `CUSTOM_MACHINE_MAX` caps the registry at 13 machines across both classes, sized so every machine that wants a character gets a select-screen icon: the select screens' icon lists, widened in place, hold 33 entries, 20 of them the vanilla roster, and a `_Static_assert` in `custom_machines.h` keeps `CKIND_NUM + CUSTOM_MACHINE_MAX` within that. The same 13 is the width the UI art banks are grown by and the width the per-machine counter arrays are relocated to, so all three move together.

Discovery runs at `OnBoot`, inside the window where hoshi has swapped `HSD_MemAlloc` and `Archive_LoadFile` for versions that allocate out of a persistent bump arena over the HSD heap. So each candidate loads through the ordinary `Archive_LoadFile`, and the mod brackets it with `HSD_ArenaMark` / `HSD_ArenaRelease` to rewind that arena once the descriptor is read. Everything the registry keeps - the strings, the rows, the generator programs, the cutscene archive names - is copied out of the descriptor first. A machine archive runs to six figures of bytes and the engine loads its own copy later, by filename, through the widened name table, so holding the boot copy would cost over a megabyte at the registry's cap for a few hundred bytes of descriptor.

A machine may drop two side-cars of the same basename next to its `.dat`: a `.ssm` sound bank, which gives it a voice of its own instead of its audio kind's, and a `.art` archive, which gives it its own picture everywhere the game draws a machine instead of the registry's placeholder.

## The archive contract

A machine is authored whole: the registry reads nothing of a vanilla machine to fill one in. Discovery reports and skips an archive that breaks any of these terms:

- **Two publics.** `customMachine`, and the `vcData` its `symbol` names, in the same archive, with all seven of that `vcData`'s pointers set and a model root under its model data.
- **`magic` `CMCH`, the current `version`, and an `is_bike` of 0 or 1.**
- **A path under 64 characters, and a name and `symbol` under 32.** Both are copied into the registry, and one cut short would name a different symbol or a name no consumer could look up. A descriptor with no name registers under its filename.
- **A name no earlier machine took**, since the name is the one handle a consumer finds a machine by.
- **`audio_kind` is a `MachineKind` of the machine's own class**: 0-18 for a star, 19-25 for a bike.
- **`stat_rows` and `cpu` are both present.** There is no engine row to fall back on.
- **A `rider_kind` that is a `RiderKind`**, when the machine wants a character.

It drops just the offending entry, and reports it, for the rest:

- **`spawn_weight` runs 0-1000.** Anything else - a negative weight would eat into every other kind's share of the roll - becomes 0.
- **A cutscene names both of its strings, `cine_file` and `cine_symbol`, each under 32 characters**, or it names neither and the machine has no cutscene; naming only one, or one too long, gets none. The archive also has to be on disc and export that public, which the cutscene's boot check confirms by loading it.
- **At most four generators, each a whole descriptor ending on its terminator.** A generator has a non-NULL `desc`, is longer than its `0x3c`-byte header (`sizeof(struct PtclDesc)`) and at most 256 bytes, header included, and its last byte is `0xfe` or `0xff`; one that is not keeps its id and emits nothing. More than four are clamped to the first four.
- **The animation bank names generators the machine can reach.** Each of its class's particle slots - seven on a star, two on a bike - is `-1`, one of the vehicle bank's own generators below 52 - shared with every machine naming it - or `52 + k` for the machine's own `k`-th generator. Anything else becomes `-1`.

`scripts/hsd/machine_descriptor.py` prints a descriptor, particle slots included, and edits one in place: `--row NAME=LOW,HIGH` and `--cpu FIELD=VALUE` set one entry, and `--audio-kind`, `--spawn-weight`, `--blip-height` and `--radar-drop` set those fields. Each edit rebuilds the descriptor's tail - its strings, rows and programs sit after it - and leaves the machine's own data where it was. Its `append` function, for a script building a machine archive, writes a descriptor onto the end of any data section, with a relocation per pointer.

```
uv run python scripts/hsd/machine_descriptor.py \
  mods/ap_star/assets/machines/VcStarAp.dat \
  --row glide_air_recover=-800,1200 --cpu swap_score=15
```

Each stat row is a `(low, high)` pair. The engine turns the stat's patch count into a ratio in `[-1, 1]` against `Patch_GetMaxValue`; below zero it weighs `low` and above zero `high` - call the one in use `pair` - and at exactly zero, every Air Ride machine without a patch, the row does nothing. A star lists six. `top_speed_ground` and `top_speed_air` are multipliers, `1 + |ratio| * (pair - 1)`. The Weight and Glide rows are added, `|ratio| * pair`: `weight_air_impulse` and `glide_air_impulse` to `vcHandlingAttr.air_impulse` (0.01 on the Slick Star), which scales the kick `Machine_Star_ApplyAirImpulse` (`0x801ebe88`) adds to the machine's acceleration in flight, and `weight_air_recover` and `glide_air_recover` to `air_recover_len` (600 on the Slick Star), the frames `Machine_Star_ApplyAirThrust` blends its thrust over. A value an order of magnitude off in an impulse row launches the machine the moment it leaves the ground. A bike lists ten, and every one multiplies: `top_speed_ground` and `top_speed_air`, then eight Turn rows, `turn_074` through `turn_0a0`, each named for the field of the bike class's shared attribute block it scales.

`blip_height` and `radar_drop` are in the units of the engine's own per-kind tables, which it scales by 0.175: how far the City Trial field blip rides over the machine - 1.87 on most stars, 2.2 on the Slick Star, 8.4 on the Wagon Star, 7.2 on the Wheelie Bike - and how far the stat radar screen lowers the machine model, 5.87 on most stars and 8 on the Wheelie Bike.

`mods/*/assets/` is copied to the disc root by the ordinary asset step, so any mod's `assets/machines/*.dat` lands at `machines/` on disc with no packaging change. A machine therefore ships as its own mod folder - `mods/ap_star/assets/machines/VcStarAp.dat` is the Archipelago Star's, with `VcStarAp.art` beside it - and this mod discovers both without knowing the mod exists. Nothing but a machine descriptor may go in `assets/machines/` under a `.dat` extension: the scan probes every `.dat` there for a `customMachine` public and reports the ones that have none. Side-cars carry their own extensions and are found by basename, never scanned. A machine's other archives - a cutscene's models, for instance - belong at the FST root.

Assets carrying data taken out of the disc - a machine archive, `CmUiFrames.dat` - are `.gitignore`d rather than committed, so no vanilla data lives in the repo. `CmUiFrames.dat` is written by its own script out of an `iso/` extraction, a machine archive is the source of truth and edited in place - its model through `scripts/hsd/builder.py`, its descriptor through `machine_descriptor.py` - and the ordinary asset step picks either up like any other file in `assets/`.

## What a machine archive holds

The `vcData` public is the seven-pointer struct hoshi declares as `vcData` in `machine.h`: attributes, model data, collision group, collision attributes, collision sphere, handling attributes and animation bank. Three of those are worth knowing about here.

The attributes block is 0x1F0 bytes in either class and `Machine_AdjustAttributes` (`0x801c7278`) memcpys it to `MachineData+0x460`, so its named fields - the rider sit bone index, base HP, ground and air top speed, the glide parameters, the quick-spin tornado size - are read live off the machine rather than off the archive. The model data holds the main model root, the bone count, six LOD table slots (main/boost x high/mid/low) and a shadow root. The collision attributes are a 0x38-byte analytic float table; there is no collision mesh anywhere in the archive.

The animation bank is the class's own. A star's `vcAnimationStar` pairs a FigaTree and a MatAnim per machine state and also carries the particle spawn ids and bones and the boost and after-boost sound ids. A bike's `vcAnimationWheel` is four such pairs, a cruise and a boost generator id, four particle bones and the cruise emitter's two timers.

Animations live inside the machine's own archive as anonymous `AnimJoint` trees, at a **0x0C stride** for machines. Five of a star bank's slots carry the machine's own motion:

| Slot | Content |
|---|---|
| Moving | idle and driving, played faster as speed rises, so give it generous frames |
| Charge | at least 100 frames; the charge gauge is a 0-100 meter and frames past 100 loop |
| Unk1 | the boost animation proper |
| Boost | the idle *during* boost |
| Stop | stop and brake |

Quick spin has no bank slot at all. A machine that wants to deform during one has to drive it from mod code off `Rider_QuickSpin_Enter` (`0x801b7ee4`), which also keeps it in sync with the spin's real duration.

## Authoring a star model

Joints 0-5 are the armature root chain every star machine opens with, and **they must carry no animation keys** - keys there break quick spin and vehicle rotation. Joint 6 is the body root, where every star machine puts its body, and the rider's seat is wherever the attributes block's sit bone index points. Bone count is the highest joint index plus one; a wrong count is the usual cause of parts detaching and the rider lagging on bad landings.

Colors can be flat material colors with one MObj per part, so no body texture is required. Delete any Blender color attributes - they make imported geometry render gray. The shadow is a separate flat top-down silhouette with normals up, solidified downward, imported into the shadow root with constant color on and diffuse shading off.

Export as glTF 2.0: its node TRS animation channels map almost one to one onto FigaTree `TRAX/Y/Z`, `ROTX/Y/Z` and `SCAX/Y/Z` tracks, which dodges the dropped-scale-keyframe problem the SMD path has. `scripts/hsd/fobj.py` is the packed-keyframe codec those tracks need, decode and encode.

## The engine tables it widens

The engine addresses a machine as a `(is_bike, class slot)` pair. Stars are slots 0-18 and their slot equals their `MachineKind`; bikes are slots 0-6 with `MachineKind` `19 + slot`. A registered machine takes the next appended slot of its class and the next `MachineKind` past 25, so its `MachineKind` is not its class slot plus either class's base - which is why consumers must resolve kinds through the API rather than through hoshi's `MachineKind_ClassIndex` / `MachineKind_FromClassIndex` (those describe vanilla and are still correct for it). The engine's own fold, `Machine_GetAbsoluteKind` (`0x801c85bc`, `is_bike ? class slot + 19 : class slot`), lands an appended star slot on the bike range and an appended bike slot on the custom kinds, so every reader of it a custom machine can reach is replaced below. Patches that only a class's machines reach are installed only when that class has one.

**Machine classes (`machine_registry.c`).** Each class grows by `CUSTOM_MACHINE_MAX` slots.

- The per-class `{filename, symbol}` name table is `stc_vcNameTable[is_bike]` (`r13 - 0x6150`). The mod copies each class's vanilla pairs - 19 star, 7 bike - into wider tables, appends each registered machine's path and symbol at its slot, and repoints both rows. The class-shared archives' own pairs live in a separate table, `stc_vcClassNameTable` (`0x804b07e0`).
- `stc_vcDataLookup` (`0x8055a068`, `vcData *[2][19]`) is relocated into a mod-owned `[2][19 + N]`, the bike row as wide as the star row. Exactly three sites read it. Two are replaced outright - `vcData_InitLookup` (`0x801c6c68`) and `Vehile_LoadFile` (`0x801c6d74`), both small. The third, `Machine_StoreVcDataPtr` (`0x801c4f98`), is patched instruction by instruction instead: its `lis` at `0x801c4fd0`, its `addi` at `0x801c4fe8` and its row stride `mulli` at `0x801c5034`. A hook there would clobber caller-saved registers the surrounding code still needs.
- `MachineDesc_SetKindAndIsBikeFromMachineKind` (`0x801c857c`) is the one canonical `MachineKind -> (is_bike, slot)` split, with 14 callers including the City Trial spawn path. Replacing it teaches all of them about the appended kinds at once.
- `Machine_EncodeVehicleKind` (`0x801c85a8`) is the reverse fold, adding 19 to a bike's slot only, so an appended star slot would read as a bike and an appended bike slot as some other custom kind. Its callers are City Trial's Free Run machine counts - reached from `Machine_Destroy` and the Wheel and Bird abilities too - two star-class compares against Hydra and Bulk, and `Ply_GetVehicleKind` (`0x8022c910`), which the Free Run lap and time attack checklist checks compare against a vanilla kind and the race finish marker (`0x80010dac`) switches on for its height. It is replaced to answer the widened `MachineKind`, which none of those compares match.
- City Trial preloads machines by walking a 26-byte enable table at `0x804b07f0` inside `Machine_PreloadAll` (`0x801c8cec`). A hook on the branch out of that loop (`0x801c8d8c`) preloads the registered archives too.
- Two 19-entry tables of machine-specific handlers, `0x804b15c0` and `0x804b160c`, sit back to back and end exactly where the star class's own descriptor begins. `Machine_Star_Init` (`0x801e7f3c`) and `Machine_Star_Think` (`0x801eacbc`) each end by loading their table at `MachineData.kind` and calling the result if it is non-NULL, with no bounds check - so slot 19 reads the descriptor's first word and branches into rodata. Only Hydra, Formula, Wagon and Turbo have handlers. Each table is read by exactly one site that does nothing but form the address, so both are relocated by rewriting the reader's `lis`/`addi` pair: `0x801e80dc`/`0x801e80e0` and `0x801eb524`/`0x801eb528`. A custom slot starts NULL, and a consumer installs its own handler there through `SetInitHandler` / `SetThinkHandler`.
- The bike class has no such tables: `Machine_Wheel_Init` (`0x801f3b54`) and `Machine_Wheel_Think` (`0x801f5390`), entries `+0x10` and `+0x38` of its class descriptor at `0x804b1c40`, dispatch through nothing indexed by kind. A bike's handlers are therefore the mod's own, run from hooks on the two epilogues - `0x801f3c70`, with the machine in `r31`, and `0x801f597c`, with it in `r29`.
- `Machine_AnimThink` (`0x801c618c`) runs once per machine per frame for both classes and ends by calling `Machine_ColAnimThink` (`0x801d60d8`) at `0x801c6274`, which reapplies the ColAnim overlays: a material color written before that call can be overwritten, and one written after it is what draws. Nothing there is indexed by kind, so the call is replaced by one that runs it and then the kind's Anim handler, which a consumer installs through `SetAnimHandler`. The handlers are held in registry order and start NULL.
- `Machine_CheckStuck` (`0x801d2b04`) leaves Dragoon, Hydra and Wing Meta Knight out of City Trial's stuck check by comparing the class slot alone against 8, 4 and 17, whatever the class, and an appended bike can sit at 8 or 17. A hook on the first compare (`0x801d2ba8`, the machine in `r29`, the slot just loaded in `r0`) hands the compares an appended slot as `0xFF`, which matches none of them. Vanilla's Wheelie Scooter, bike slot 4, still matches Hydra's 4 and still skips the check.

**Patch-stat scaling (`machine_stat_scaling.c`).** `Machine_ApplyStarStatScaling` (`0x801e81e4`) scales a machine's attributes by its patch stats out of `(low, high)` pairs in the class table `MachineData.stat_scale` points at, a block of `VcStar.dat`. Most pairs are one per class, but six are kept one row per kind, in 19-row blocks: Top Speed's two at `+0x068` and `+0x100`, Weight's at `+0x328` and `+0x3c0`, Glide's at `+0x538` and `+0x5d0`. Each is read as `lbz kind` / `slwi 3` / `addi block` / `add` with no bound, so slot 19 would read the first row of the block after its own - for Weight and Glide, rows sized for `air_recover_len` (hundreds of frames) landing on a 0.01 `air_impulse`, which throws the machine off any ledge once either stat moves off zero. No other reader of the class table indexes it by kind.

The six reads are rewritten in place, starting at `0x801e83c0`, `0x801e83e4`, `0x801e8f38`, `0x801e8f64`, `0x801e8fc8` and `0x801e9008`, into a `bl` to a resolver taking the machine and the row. A vanilla slot gets exactly the pair the engine would have read; a custom slot gets its descriptor's `stat_rows`, which discovery requires. Rewriting beats replacing the function: it is `0xe6c` bytes with some sixty scale sites, and only these six depend on the machine. The call is safe where it lands because the stat ratio is held in `f31` and both table bases in `r29` / `r31`, so it only has to put the ratio back in `f1` for the `Machine_ScaleFromRatio` call that follows. The Weight and Glide reads interleave that ratio save and the `md->attr` reload with the four index instructions, and those two move ahead of the call. Two of the windows begin at a branch target, which is harmless since each rewrite is self-contained from its first instruction.

`Machine_ApplyBikeStatScaling` (`0x801f3d44`) keeps ten such blocks of 7 rows in `VcWheel.dat`'s class table: Top Speed's two at `+0x060` and `+0x098`, and eight Turn blocks from `+0x128` to `+0x2b0`, `0x38` apart, each scaling a field of the bike class's shared attribute block. All ten go through `Machine_ScaleFromRatio`, so every bike row multiplies. The air Top Speed read (`0x801f3fb4`) and the eight Turn reads (`0x28` apart from `0x801f41d8`) take the same rewrite, each Turn read's `md->attr` reload into `r29` moved ahead of the call. The ground Top Speed read at `0x801f3f90` saves the ratio into `f31` inside its own four instructions, leaving no room to put the ratio back after a call, so that window becomes the ratio save, the two argument loads and a call that returns the scaled result itself, and the engine's `bl Machine_ScaleFromRatio` after it becomes a no-op.

**CPU rows (`machine_cpu.c`).** CPU riders read three per-machine DOL tables and switch on the machine's kind in a few more places, and every one of those reaches the kind through `Machine_GetAbsoluteKind`: left to the engine, a custom machine would be driven, and judged from the outside, by another machine's rows or by whatever lies past the tables. The descriptor authors a `CustomMachineCpu` instead, and every reader is replaced with one that hands a vanilla machine its engine row and a custom machine its authored fields:

| Engine data | Readers | Fields |
|---|---|---|
| `CpuMachineCaps`, `0x804b8854` (19 star rows) and `0x804b89d0` (7 bike rows), stride `0x14` | `Machine_CPUGetSwapScore`, `Machine_CPUCanSwap`, `Machine_CPUCanBrake`, `Machine_CPUCanChargeHold`, `Machine_CPUCanRamCharge`, `Machine_CPUSkipsPassageBranch`, `Machine_CPUGetChargeRelease` (`0x8027699c`-`0x80276d1c`) | `swap_score`, `flags`, `charge_release` |
| `CpuMachineSteer`, `0x804b8f30`, star rows only - bikes read the Warp Star's | `Rider_CPUGetMachineAlignCosNear`, `AlignCosFar`, `StuckAngle` (`0x80277538`-`0x80277640`), `Rider_CPUGetMachineTurnTolerance` (`0x802776c4`) | `align_cos_near`, `align_cos_far`, `stuck_angle`, `turn_tolerance` |
| Air Glider and High Jump `{pitch, min_len}` pairs, `0x804b8a5c` / `0x804b8b24`, 25 each by absolute kind | `Rider_CPUGetAirGliderMachineParam` / `Rider_CPUGetHighJumpMachineParam` (`0x80277024` / `0x80277094`) | `air_glider_*`, `high_jump_*` |
| The charge-hold gate Bulk, Hydra, Rocket and Formula carry | `Machine_CPUGetChargeHoldGate` (`0x80276de4`) | `has_charge_hold_gate`, `charge_hold_gate` |
| The release level Bulk and Hydra carry | `Machine_CPUGetChargeReleaseOverride` (`0x80276ea0`) | `has_release_level`, `release_level` |
| Hydra's held-up stick, Winged and Jet Star's dive once moving | `CpuData.machine_kind`, compared in `Rider_CPUEmitSteerStick` | `stick_pitch` |

All sixteen accessors are reached only through a plain `bl`, so each is replaced outright. The replacements hand a vanilla machine its row as the engine read it: the caps and steer tables by `(is_bike, class slot)`, which is the row the engine's own absolute-kind-and-split path reached, and the Air Glider and High Jump pairs and both kind switches still through `Machine_GetAbsoluteKind`, which is exact for a vanilla machine. The switches' constants are loaded by address, as hoshi's `stc_cpu_charge_gate_*` and `stc_cpu_charge_release_*`. The kind cache is the one site rewritten instead, the `bl` at `0x8026bc6c` in `Rider_ProcessCPUDistance`: its only reader compares it against Hydra, Winged and Jet Star, so a custom machine caches whichever of those flies the pitch it authors, and its own `MachineKind` otherwise. `Machine_CPUIsKindSlotFilled` (`0x80276db4`) asks whether the City Trial spawn slot numbered by the machine's absolute kind holds a machine - a vanilla mis-index kept for vanilla machines - and a custom machine asks after its own `MachineData.city_spawn_slot`. The Rex Wheelie special cases in `Rider_CPUEmitSteerStick` and `Rider_CPUArbitrateManeuver` test for bike slot 3 and pass over a custom bike.

**Per-machine counters (`machine_stats.c`).** `PlayerStats` carries three `int[0x1a]` arrays - `machine_change_count` at `+0x37c`, `kills_by_machine` at `+0x3e4` and `deaths_by_machine` at `+0x44c` - and their writers fold `(is_bike, class slot)` into an absolute `MachineKind` themselves, with no bounds check. A custom machine's fold lands wherever its appended slot sends it - an appended star on the bike half of the range, an appended bike past it - and far enough out it writes past all three arrays into `ko_cpu_machine_broken`, the three other KO-by-cause counters, `vehicle_bust_mask` and `item_collect[0]`, every one of which is a checklist condition.

There is no slack to widen them in place - the three run back to back and the five-player `stc_playerdata` block (`0x8055a9f0`, stride `0x90c`) ends 0x74 bytes short of `gEffectMgr` - so they are relocated into a mod-owned `int[5][2][26 + N]`, keyed by the widened kind. Every site that touches them is reached at a `bl`, so four call replacements cover the feature: `Ply_IncrementGetOnMachineNum` at `0x801ba190` in `AS_GetOnStar`, `Ply_AddDeath` at `0x801e1f74` in `Machine_GiveDamage`, and the two readers - `Ply_GetMachineChangeCount` at `0x8004e6a8` in `CityTrial_CheckFreeRunObjectives`, which unlocks a checklist cell at ten machine changes, and `Ply_GetKONum` at `0x80012470` in `Game_Think`, which publishes the Destruction Derby score. Both readers sum their whole array, so relocating them keeps every vanilla answer identical. Only those two arrays are mirrored: nothing reads a widened `deaths_by_machine`, so the engine's own copy is left to `Ply_AddDeath`. The round reset is done from this mod's own `On3DLoadStart`, which runs just before `Player_InitAll` clears the engine's own copies.

`Ply_AddDeath` still runs, because it also sets the KO-by-cause counters, the vehicle-bust mask and the King Dedede frame; only the counting is taken over. A kind it has no bucket for is swapped for `VCKIND_WHEELVSDEDEDE` on the way in - in range, never on the field, and named by no vehicle-bust entry, so its two remaining array writes land somewhere unread and no bust achievement can fire by accident. That one call is the only seam a KO can be seen from, so the replacement then tells every handler a consumer added.

**HUD and radar placement (`machine_hud.c`).** Three places draw or place a machine by its kind rather than its `CharacterKind`, and each reads a vanilla per-kind table with no bound. The two art banks among them grow by `CUSTOM_MACHINE_MAX` frames in registry order, as described under the UI art banks, so custom machine *n* shows frame `20 + n` in each.

The City Trial blip is the floating icon over every machine on the field, one per machine per player view. `CityBlip_Create` (`0x801226e8`) makes one template joint per `MachineKind` 0-25 out of `ScInfWarpstarct` in `IfAll2c.dat`, posed on the frame a 26-entry table at `0x804a85f8` names (`0xFFFFFFFF` for no blip), into `Game3dData.blip_template` - an array followed directly by the per-player instance joints. `CityBlip_GXCallback` (`0x80122380`) instances the template at `Machine_GetAbsoluteKind`, so a custom machine lands on another kind's template - an appended star's first two slots on wheelie-animal entries with no blip, the next five on a real bike's icon - or past the array on the instance joints, which the callback then instances into themselves until `JObj_DispAll` recurses without end. So a custom machine's template lives in the mod. A hook past `CityBlip_Create`'s template loop (`0x80122840`) builds one per registered machine exactly as the loop does - `JObj_LoadJoint`, `JObj_AddSetAnim0_SetFrameAndRate` (`0x80114d9c`) on its appended frame, the same joint and material flags - and a hook on `CityBlip_Destroy`'s tail (`0x80122b20`) frees them once the vanilla templates and instances are gone. The callback's `bl Machine_GetAbsoluteKind` becomes a call returning the template itself, and the three instructions after it that indexed the array become `mr r22, r3` and two no-ops. The hook clears the custom template array before it checks whether the blip bank grew on this load, so a scene torn down without reaching `CityBlip_Destroy` leaves no joint off its freed heap behind; when the bank did not grow no custom template is built, so the machine goes without a blip rather than wearing another machine's frame.

How high the blip rides is its own lookup. `CityBlip_GetMachineLift` (`0x800096b8`), called only from the blip's per-view placement at `0x80122c2c`, copies a 19-entry star float table, `stc_blip_lift_star` (`0x804894a0`), and a 7-entry bike table, `stc_blip_lift_bike` (`0x804894ec`), onto its stack, reads one by class slot and scales it by `stc_blip_lift_scale`'s 0.175, so an appended slot reads the other table or the caller's frame. It is replaced outright: a vanilla slot gets the engine's float, a custom machine its descriptor's `blip_height`.

The stadium HUD's machine icon - High Jump's opponent heights, the flight-distance marker and the stadium icon element - takes its frame from `Machine_GetIconFrame` (`0x8011584c`), a byte out of the 17-byte `stc_machine_icon_frame` (`0x804a7b84`) read at `is_bike * MACHINE_ICON_FRAME_BIKE_BASE + class slot`, that base being 12, which puts the vanilla bikes at 12 and up, where an appended star slot would also land. It is replaced outright: a vanilla machine gets its table byte, a custom machine its appended frame. The frames are `ScInfStarIcon`'s TexAnim in `IfAll3c.dat`, 40x40 C4 like the menu icons, so a machine's `icon` art fills both.

`MnRadar_PlaceMachines` (`0x80045e14`) places each player's machine model on the City Trial stat radar screen, lowering it by a float out of a 19-entry star table at `0x80489558` or a 7-entry bike table at `0x804895a4`, read at the player's saved `(is_bike, class slot)` - `GameData.city.is_bike[ply]` and `GameData.city.machine_kind[ply]`, at `GameData+0x454` and `+0x459` - and scaled by 0.175. Both tables are copied onto the stack first, so an appended slot reads the frame's other locals. A hook on the scale load after the read (`0x80045ffc`) takes the player from `r30` and the value read from `f2`, reads the player's pair out of `GameData` itself, and swaps in a custom machine's `radar_drop`; everything the function uses past that point is callee-saved or reloaded, so the call clobbers nothing live.

**Audio (`machine_audio.c`).** `(*stc_machineAudioParams)->params[is_bike]` is each class's array of 0x94-byte `MachineAudioParams`, authored in `VcCommon.dat` - 19 star rows, 7 bike rows - and indexed by class slot at 17 sites, none bounded. A hook on `vcLoadCommon`'s epilogue (`0x801c6d64`) copies both classes' vanilla rows into wider mod-owned arrays, fills each custom slot from its descriptor's `audio_kind`, and repoints both. `VcCommon.dat` reloads per scene, so this runs on every scene entry; the guard only stops a second splice of the mod's own arrays within one load.

That row opens with thirteen FGM ids, and they are the whole of a machine's voice. The Slick Star's, as an example of a star's:

A machine that wants nothing new can simply pick different ids out of that vocabulary; the vanilla samples are `iso/files/audio/jp/star.ssm` with routing in `airride.sem`, and `iso/files/audio/adfgmnametable.dat` names them. Keep the row's idle volume floor at 0.0: a nonzero floor clamps to full volume and makes the machine hum while parked, as the Wagon Star's does.

Each class keeps one more per-kind table outside that row: its sound table, which the class archive's `vcDataKindStar` carries at `+0x0c` and the class's attribute copy hands every machine as `MachineData.x654`. The star's holds the air-noise loop's FGM id per slot from `+0x30` - `SFX_airnoize_s/m/l_lp`, which `Machine_SoundEffectThink` (`0x801ee588`) starts at the volume `Machine_Star_ApplyGrip` (`0x801ebc90`) leaves in `MachineData+0xc44`, written at `0x801ebe58`. The bike's holds a `{loop, one-shot}` pair per slot from `+0x1c`, which the Wheel class's sound callbacks at `0x801f87a8` and `0x801f8874` play. Both are indexed with no bound, and nothing reads the table but the two attribute copies, so the `Vehile_LoadFile` replacement widens it as data the moment the class archive loads: the header and vanilla entries are copied into a mod-owned table, each custom slot takes its `audio_kind`'s entry, and the class's pointer is repointed, so every machine created that scene gets the wider table. No `.ssm` role supplies either.

**Characters (`character_registry.c`).** Four DOL tables describe the roster and sit back to back with no slack: the select-screen linear strip `stc_selicon_ckind_linear` (`0x804957ec`, 20 bytes), the select-screen grid `stc_selicon_ckind_grid` (`0x80495800`, 2x10), the `CharacterDesc` table `stc_character_desc` (`0x80495814`, 20x3) and the star class's slot-to-`CharacterKind` map `stc_machine_ckind_star` (`0x80495850`, 19 bytes). The first three are each read by exactly one accessor that does nothing but form an address, so they are relocated by rewriting the `lis`/`addi` pair inside the accessor - no hook, no register hazards:

| Accessor | `lis` | `addi` |
|---|---|---|
| `SelIcon_GetCKindLinear` | `0x8000b9a8` | `0x8000b9b0` |
| `SelIcon_GetCKind` | `0x8000b9c0` | `0x8000b9cc` |
| `Character_GetDesc` | `0x8000b9e0` | `0x8000b9e8` |

`Machine_GetCKind` (`0x8000b9f4`) reads the fourth. It is the reverse of `CharacterDesc_GetMachineKind`: a `(is_bike, class slot)` pair back to the `CharacterKind` whose art to draw, over two byte tables - the star one above and the 7-byte `stc_machine_ckind_bike` at `r13 - 0x7fbc`, which no `lis`/`addi` pair forms - so it is replaced outright. Fifteen call sites go through it, all on the four results screens and the time-attack board; the select screens hold the `CharacterKind` already and never touch it. Vanilla parks its own art-less machines - the Free, Steer and two wing stars on the Warp Star, the plain Wheel, Wheel Kirby and both Dedede bikes on the Wheelie Scooter - and a custom slot with no `CharacterKind` behind it goes where its class parks them. Left to the engine, an appended star slot reads past the star table into zeros and every results screen draws the Compact Star, and an appended bike slot reads the string that follows the bike table.

The grid gains a column per two appended characters, and its runtime row stride is written into `SelIcon_GetCKind`'s `mulli r5, r0, cols` at `0x8000b9c4`. Appended characters fill the new columns row 0 first, so a single addition gives an 11/10 grid rather than 10/11. At most one cell is left over; it holds a sentinel `CharacterKind` one past the last real one, which indexes a zeroed `CharacterDesc` row and which every availability predicate rejects, so it can never be packed into a select list. The appended `CharacterDesc` row is `{rider_kind, is_bike, class slot}` - class-relative, as every vanilla row is.

`CUSTOM_MACHINE_MAX` is sized so every machine that wants a character gets one: the 20 vanilla characters plus 13 is the 33 icons a select screen carries. Art never gates a cell: the side-car authors its ramps a fixed `CUSTOM_MACHINE_MAX` frames wide whether or not a machine claims each slot, and an unclaimed slot holds the placeholder, so a machine is never refused a cell for want of a portrait.

**Select screens (`select_screen.c`).** This mod both widens the screens and packs them, because both screens build their icon lists by walking the character grid it widened. Vanilla's packing loops are a hard 10 columns into two 10-byte stack rows, so an 11th column has nowhere to land and an appended character stays invisible however well it is registered.

Four things cap a select screen at 20 icons, and all four are moved here, to the 33 of `SELECT_ICON_MAX`:

- The packed icon list in `GameData` - a count at select base `+0x65` and one `CharacterKind` per icon from `+0x66`, off `GameData+0x10a` on Air Ride and `+0x1d0` on City Trial - is followed immediately by live bytes: vanilla keeps Air Ride's row-layout flag at `+0x7a` and its debug-grid flag at `+0x7b`, and City Trial's debug-grid flag at `+0x7a`. Base `+0x11` to `+0x20` on both screens is 16 bytes no engine code reads - only the block memsets touch them, `CSS_airRide_InitSelectData` clearing 0x8d bytes and `CitySelect_InitSelectData` 0x8c - so the three flags move there, by rewriting the displacement of every instruction that reaches them. Air Ride's row-split goes to `+0x11` (`GameData+0x11b`) across five base-relative `lbz`/`stb` (`0x80027f60`, `0x800285c8`, `0x80028818`, `0x80028970`, `0x80029c7c`). Its debug-grid goes to `+0x12` (`GameData+0x11c`) across five (`0x8002881c`, `0x8002895c`, `0x80028968`, `0x80029c64`, `0x80029c70`) plus one relative to `GameData` itself, `lbz r0, 0x185(r3)` at `0x800216d8` in `CSS_airRide_colorChanger`. City Trial's goes to `+0x11` (`GameData+0x1e1`) across six (`0x8002e444`, `0x80038d00`, `0x8003a150`, `0x8003a15c`, `0x8003ac3c`, `0x8003ac48`) plus two relative to `GameData`: `lbz r0, 0x24a(r3)` at `0x8002f2bc` in `CitySelect_ChangeColor`, and `CitySelect_LoadCityTrial`'s clear, `stb r0, 0x24a(r3)` at `0x80038d98`. The two colour-changer reads are the debug grid's colour bypass: while the flag is 1 the changer treats every Kirby colour as unlocked. The sites inside `AirRide_PopulateSelectIcons` are left alone, because the whole function is replaced.

  Packing a list clears only the vanilla list and the flag bytes it vacated - 22 bytes on Air Ride (`+0x66` to `+0x7b`), 21 on City Trial (`+0x66` to `+0x7a`). The list can still take 33 entries, and entries past the 22nd on Air Ride or the 21st on City Trial land on the four per-slot 4-byte arrays after it - Air Ride's at `+0x7c`, `+0x80`, `+0x84` and `+0x88`, City Trial's at `+0x7b`, `+0x7f`, `+0x83` and `+0x87`. Those are controller-claim state for multi-console (LAN) play: a per-console claimed-pad bitmask, each slot's ordinal on its console, each slot's console index and each slot's claimed pad (-1 for none). `CSS_airRide_ModeDispatch` (from `0x8002a220`) and `CitySelect_MinorLoad` (from `0x8003b330`) initialise them before the list is built, and every other reader and writer sits behind the engine's session check, the call hoshi names `Checklist_IsCacheValid` (`0x8007b650`). The list is built exactly once per screen load, always after that initialisation, and no engine code reads a list entry past the count, so an overflowing list is harmless offline and breaks controller claiming only in LAN play.

- The icon positions come from a strip of 20 anchor joints posed by an animation whose frame is the icon count, which has no key past 20. `AirRideSelect_LayoutIcons` and `CitySelect_LayoutMachineIcons` are replaced: they call the engine's pass, which is exact up to 20 icons, and past that redo the grid arithmetically - `spacing = 2H / max(ceil(N/2) - 1, floor(N/2) - 0.5)`, with the shared icon scale multiplied by the spacing ratio so the tiles still fit their columns. Icons with no anchor are held in the mod, because the ipos userdata - `AirRideSelectIposData` / `CitySelectIposData` in hoshi's `scene.h`, 20 anchor pointers at `+0x10` and 20 `Vec3`s at `+0x60` - ends its positions flush against Air Ride's shared scale at `+0x150` and City Trial's count at `+0x150` and scale at `+0x154`; `AirRideSelect_GetIconPos` and `CitySelect_GetIconPos` are replaced to hand them out. The rows stay at two however many icons there are - the engine's cursor navigation is built for two - so the extra icons are absorbed by tightening the spacing rather than by adding a third row.

  The half-width `H`, the two row heights and the block's centre are measured off the strip on arrival rather than named as constants. The engine's pass fills the userdata array by calling `JObj_GetWorldPosition` on each anchor, and the two screens differ in what that returns: City Trial's anchors carry the layout animation's scale themselves, so a world position is the authored coordinate, while Air Ride's twenty hang under one parent joint the animation scales - 1.0 up to five icons, 0.82 from ten on - so its authored `-30.607` reaches the array as `-25.10`. Past 20 icons the animation holds its twenty-icon pose, so the strip always arrives holding that layout: `H` and the centre come from the first and last anchor, the row heights from the first of each row.
- The icon GObj array in `ScMenuCommon` holds 20 pointers and its writer indexes it unguarded, so an icon past the strip walks into the scene-model pointers that follow - by the seventh, the ipos GObj the layout above reads its positions out of. Both creators end in the same four instructions, an `extsb`/`slwi`/`add`/`stw` immediately before the epilogue, so the store itself is taken over: a hook at `0x8015181c` (Air Ride) and `0x8015c2dc` (City Trial) exits past it at `0x8015182c` / `0x8015c2ec`, keeping indices under 20 in `ScMenuCommon.airride_select.sicon_gobj` / `city_select.sicon_gobj` and dropping appended ones. Nothing reads either array.
- The packing itself. `AirRide_PopulateSelectIcons` (`0x80020a08`) is replaced outright. City Trial's lives inside `CitySelect_CreateMachineIcons` (`0x8002e3c4`), whose Stadium and Free Run passes count the roster, pack the vanilla grid and reorder it assuming the vanilla grid's fixed positions for the legendary machines, which duplicates icons on a packed list. The instructions at `0x8002e4d0` (Stadium) and `0x8002e5c0` (Free Run) each become `b 0x8002f0b8` (`0x48000be8` and `0x48000af8`), skipping all of that straight to a hook on the tail at `0x8002f0b8`, which packs the list, lays it out and creates the icons, returns the count in r27 for the `stb r27, 0x65(r30)` it displaced, and exits to `0x8002f220`.

An appended character never reaches `AirRide_CheckCharacterAvailable`, whose 20-entry jump table has no arm for one: its only engine callers are inside the replaced `AirRide_PopulateSelectIcons`, and the packing asks it about vanilla kinds only.

One patch is not about widening. `CitySelect_Cursor1InputThink` splits cursor rows at `num >= 10` (`cmpwi r3, 9; ble` at `0x80031350`), while the grid renderer keeps up to 10 icons on one drawn row and only wraps at 11 - so at exactly 10 the cursor splits 5+5 across a single row. Vanilla City Trial only ever produces counts 15-20; a filtered roster can land on 10, so the compare becomes `cmpwi r3, 10`.

A consumer that gates characters registers a `CustomMachineAvailabilityFilter` through `SetAvailabilityFilter` instead of replacing the packing. The filter receives the candidate and the engine's own answer for it, so it can narrow the roster (`return default && mine`) or ignore the engine entirely. The grid's one leftover cell is safe to ignore: its sentinel sits at or past `GetCharacterKindCeiling()`, which the predicate rejects before the filter ever sees it.

## Drop-in audio

A machine that wants a voice of its own ships a `.ssm` sound bank beside its `.dat`, same basename - `machines/VcMine.dat` and `machines/VcMine.ssm`. Nothing on the disc is replaced and the machine archive is untouched; a machine with no companion keeps its audio kind's sounds.

The bank is an ordinary HAL sound bank holding exactly one record per row slot, in the order above. A slot the author does not supply is a record with a sample rate of 0 and no samples, and that slot keeps the audio kind's id. Records may point into the same data, so a machine whose engine start is its engine loop - which is what every vanilla star does - pays for one copy.

Three things have to line up before one of those samples can play, and `machine_audio.c` sets all three up on the first `vcLoadCommon`, by which point the audio system is running and the DVD is available:

- **The samples have to be in ARAM.** `FGM_GetNextLargestSSMSizeIndex` (`0x80448274`) carves one SSM slot big enough for every companion bank, and each is loaded into it with `FGM_QueueLoad` (`0x8044809c`) plus `FGM_SychronousLoad` (`0x80448220`). Slots are carved for the run of the game, so this happens once; retail leaves about 1.48 MiB of the ARAM sample arena free.
- **They need global sound indices no vanilla bank claims.** A sample is addressed by an index that runs across every bank on the disc, and the vanilla banks tile 0-614 with no gap. A bank declares its own base in its header, which an author cannot choose safely once there is more than one companion bank, so the mod assigns it: the first starts at 615 and each one after it starts past the last. `FGM_LoadBankCallback` (`0x80447ea4`) queues the read callback `FGM_UnkCallback` (`0x80447a74`), which takes that number out of the staged file header (`stc_ssm_load_header`, read at `0x80447cc4`). A hook at `0x80447eb8` - `FGM_LoadBankCallback+0x14`, past the prologue, where the DVD callback's arguments are dead - overwrites the staged base before `FGM_UnkCallback` runs, while the bank's own load is the only one in flight. The ceiling is hoshi's `SSM_VANILLA_SOUND_NUM`, a constant rather than a scan of what is loaded, because banks come and go per scene. `Audio_AllocPID` (`0x80448f08`) resolves an index through a chained hash and drops the sound on a miss, so appended indices cost nothing, but an index two banks both claim resolves to whichever one the hash chain reaches first.
- **A script has to play them.** An FGM id names a script, not a sample; the script carries the sound's volume and pitch envelope. Each drop-in sound gets a copy of whichever script the audio kind's row names for that slot, with its one `0x01` command's operand rewritten to the new index, so a drop-in engine loop carries that script's envelope. Where the audio kind leaves that slot at -1 - the three boost tiers on the Slick Star, the Wagon Star and the two wing riders - the copy is taken from the first row of the same class carrying one, so a drop-in can fill a slot its audio kind never had. Those copies go into an appended SEM bank: the script map is described entirely by `stc_fgm_bank_num`, `stc_fgm_script_num`, `stc_fgm_bank_start_script` and `stc_fgm_script_data`, so widening it is copying both tables into larger mod-owned arrays, appending bank 20 and repointing all four. Every vanilla FGM id keeps its meaning because the appended bank sits past them all. `FGM_LoadAirrideSem` (`0x8005c584`) reinstalls the vanilla map on a scene reset, so a hook at `0x8005c654` puts the appended bank back after each one. Both tables and the script copies are statically sized arrays in the mod image, because the game holds those pointers for the run of the game and `HSD_MemAlloc` only returns memory that outlives the scene when it is called from a mod's boot callback - anything allocated later is reused the moment the scene changes, and the game reads the reused bytes as script pointers.

`scripts/audio/machine_audio.py` builds a bank from `.wav` files:

```
uv run python scripts/audio/machine_audio.py build machines/VcMine.ssm \
  --engine engine_lp.wav --surface tires_lp.wav
```

Source audio is 16-bit PCM WAV at any rate; the tool encodes to the DSP-ADPCM the hardware wants, generates each sound's coefficient book, and writes the loop point and its decoder context. `roles` lists the slot names, `info` describes a built bank and `dump` writes its sounds back out as WAVs.

## The machine name and description

Moving the cursor onto an icon draws the machine's name and its description underneath. Both screens get those from their own SIS file - `SisSelply.dat` on Air Ride, `SisSelplyCt.dat` on City Trial, each loaded into SIS slot 0 and each holding exactly 48 entries: two data pointers, six pieces of screen furniture, the 20 machine names at 8-27 and their 20 descriptions at 28-47. A `CharacterKind` becomes a pair of those indices through two 20-entry tables of words read as signed bytes, and each table is read by exactly one instruction pair:

| Screen | Reader | Name table | Description table |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air Ride | `AirRideSelect_SetMachineText` (`0x80153d2c`) | `0x804aa3d8` (`lis` `0x80153d58`, `addi` `0x80153d68`) | `0x804aa428` (`lis` `0x80153d5c`, `addi` `0x80153d6c`) |
| City Trial | `CitySelect_SetMachineText` (`0x8015e740`) | `0x804aa598` (`lis` `0x8015e76c`, `addi` `0x8015e77c`) | `0x804aa5e8` (`lis` `0x8015e770`, `addi` `0x8015e780`) |

Neither table nor either SIS file has a spare entry, so an appended `CharacterKind` reads a text index out of whatever data follows the table and then dereferences past the end of the entry pointer array - a hard crash inside `Text_StorePremadeText` (`0x8044f9d4`) the moment the cursor lands on the icon. `select_text.c` relocates all four tables widened, by the same `lis`/`addi` rewrite the character tables use.

The appended rows point at entries this mod adds to the SIS array itself. `Text_InitPremadeText` resolves an index as `stc_sis_data[sis_id][index]`, so a hook on the epilogue of each screen's loader - `AirRideSelect_LoadSisFile` (`0x8013bacc`, hooked at `0x8013baf0`) and `CitySelect_LoadSisFile` (`0x8013c4a8`, hooked at `0x8013c4cc`) - copies the loaded 48 pointers into a mod-owned array with the extra entries after them and repoints slot 0 at it. That runs per load, because the array it copies lives in the scene's heap.

Entries `48 + 2n` and `48 + 2n + 1` are the *n*-th appended character's name and description, composed from its descriptor in the same SIS opcodes the vanilla entries use - the name centered, kerned, black at scale 0.5 and upper-cased since every vanilla machine name is; the description left-aligned and gray at scale 0.55 in a box 20 units down, with `\n` in the descriptor string becoming a `LINEBREAK` opcode. Vanilla blurbs are two lines of about 24 characters and `FIT_ON` squeezes anything wider, so that is the budget. A machine with no description still gets an entry, composed from an empty string: both indices have to be valid, because the screen draws neither text when either is -1.

It matches the image-count test only because it happens to hold 20 images, and its 96x24 I4 geometry is no role, so it is skipped.

Each bank grows by appended `ImageDesc`s (plus a TLUT entry apiece when a TLUT track indexes them), rebuilt image and TLUT pointer arrays, bumped counts in the TexAnim header at `+0x14`/`+0x16`, and keys on the AObj's image-index (track 1) and TLUT-index (track 10) ramps.

**How many frames.** The banks grow by a fixed 13, not by however many machines happen to be registered: the ramps are authored keyframe data, so their width is decided when the side-car is built, and 13 is `CUSTOM_MACHINE_MAX`. Unclaimed slots hold the placeholder and no CharacterKind or registered machine ever reaches them. The widest bank therefore goes 34 -> 47.

**Where the frames go.** The portrait, icon and rule banks index frame for frame. The silhouette and picture banks do not: the engine diverts King Dedede to frame `20 + color` and Meta Knight to `30 + color`, so the frames an appended CharacterKind needs - 20 through 32 - are already Dedede's and would run into Meta Knight's. Both runs move up by the appended count instead, to 33-40 and 43-50, and `ui_frames.c` rewrites the sixteen `addi` immediates that form them. Dedede's eight sit at `0x80151b08` in `AirRideSelect_SetSIcon2Color`, `0x80151bd4` in `AirRideSelect_SetSIcon2Character`, `0x8015c5c8` in `CitySelect_SetSIcon2Color`, `0x8015c694` in `CitySelect_SetSIcon2Character`, and `0x801672bc`, `0x8016b064`, `0x8016e9bc` and `0x80177b5c` in the four results screens' `CreateSiconBig`; Meta Knight's are `0x14` past each of those, in the same if/else chain. Those patches apply whether or not a drop-in machine was found, because the splice runs either way - and they are why every bank a patched site drives has to be grown together with the rest.

A vanilla ramp is a `u8` over a `2^3` divisor, which tops out at 31.875 and cannot hold an image index past 31. The values are whole numbers, so the exponent is dropped as far as the new keys need and the format - and with it the buffer length - stays as it was. The fitted `value_flag` travels in the side-car alongside the buffer.

**The quad they are drawn on.** A picture bank's frames are not all the same shape. Frames `0` to `17` run 72x64, 80x48, 64x72 and 80x64; King Dedede's is 56x80 and Meta Knight's 104x52. Nothing in an `ImageDesc` carries an aspect, so the Sicon models size the quad per character instead, through a pair of `SCAX`/`SCAY` `AnimJoint` tracks on the same timeline as the image ramp - the value being the frame's pixel dimensions times about 0.104. Six of the eight menu archives carry four such tracks apiece, two joints for the picture and the silhouette drawn under it; `MnBestrapAll.dat` and `MnSelstadiumAll.dat` carry none, their icons all being 40x40.

Those tracks have to grow with the image ramps. Left alone, an appended frame draws at the shape of whatever frame it displaced - the appended run takes Dedede's tall 56x80 quad and Dedede, moved to frame 33, falls past the last key into Meta Knight's wide 104x52 one. They widen the same way, except that the new keys hold a constant rather than a running index: the source frame's scale, which is the shape every appended frame is encoded at. They are found by the same test that identifies a picture bank, applied to every `AnimJoint` ramp in the archive rather than to the `TexAnim`'s, and travel in the side-car as a per-archive list of `(FObjDesc offset, buffer, length, value_flag)` beside the banks. `ScMenSelplyIpos`'s anchor tracks key frames 18 and 19 and none at 30, so the test passes over them.

**Where the art comes from.** Growth is applied to the loaded archives rather than shipped as rewritten copies of them; rewriting on disc would mean carrying 3.9 MB of vanilla archive to deliver 86 KB of additions. Only two of the four edits are position-independent and can be baked: an `ImageDesc` with its texels, and a replacement keyframe buffer per ramp. The pointer arrays hold the archive's own descriptors, which move with every load, so they are rebuilt at runtime into a static pool with a fixed slot per bank.

`CmUiFrames.dat` carries one placeholder image per bank, the widened ramps and the widened quad-scale tracks. Twenty-two banks need only five distinct textures between them and most of their ramps encode to the same bytes, so both are interned and the side-car comes to 27 KB. A registered machine's own art overrides the placeholder in that machine's own slot, and travels beside the machine as `machines/<name>.art` - an archive exporting a `customMachineArt` public, magic `CMAR`, holding one image per bank geometry keyed by the source frame's `(width, height, format)`. A machine with no side-car shows the placeholder, so it still gets a cell.

```
uv run --with pillow python scripts/authoring/make_ui_frames.py

uv run --with pillow python scripts/hsd/make_machine_art.py \
  mods/ap_star/assets/machines/VcStarAp.art \
  --hero art/ap-star-hero.png --topdown art/ap-star-top.png --matte auto
```

Two renders are needed because the icon's angle is not the picture's: a three-quarter hero view from slightly above, and a straight top-down. Both want a generous margin and at least 4x the target resolution - 320x192 or better for the 80x48 slots. Each is cropped to its own alpha before anything is framed, so the margin costs nothing and a subject sitting small in a big canvas still fills its target. Art is then scaled to fit and padded, never stretched or cropped.

A render off a model viewer usually arrives opaque on the viewport's backdrop instead. `--matte RRGGBB` keys a flat one out, and `--matte auto` reads the colour off the four corners and fails if they disagree. Alpha comes from how far a pixel travels from that colour and the backdrop's share is divided back out, so an additive glow keeps its falloff rather than ending on the hard edge a colour-equality key leaves. `--preview <path>` writes the finished images magnified side by side, which is the only way to judge a 32x32 blip or 40x40 icon before it is on screen.

**Getting the renders.** A machine archive does not open in a model viewer as it ships. HSDraw types a root by its public name's suffix - `_joint` is a JObj, `_matanim_joint` a MatAnimJoint tree, `_figatree` a FigaTree - and a machine exports one public, a `vcData<Class><Stem>`, which names nothing the viewer can draw. `scripts/hsd/machine_preview.py` writes a viewer-only copy: the same data section and relocations under a public table naming the model root, the shadow model and each animation-bank slot the way the viewer expects. Every joint carries its three LOD meshes at once and a viewer draws all of them, so it also keeps only the DObjs one LOD table asks for - `--lod`, high by default - and a DObj every table lists survives with whichever is chosen. The copy is not a disc asset: pruning leaves the LOD tables indexing a flat DObj order the tree no longer has, and nothing binds a machine by these names.

```
uv run python scripts/hsd/machine_preview.py mods/ap_star/assets/machines/VcStarAp.dat
```

It defaults to `out/preview/<stem>_preview.dat`, which `make clean` takes with the rest of `out/`.

To light the render the way City Trial does, `grDataCity1`'s primary light chain is a white ambient plus one INFINITE diffuse+specular light coloured `(255, 255, 217)` at `(-1000, 700, 1500)` with no interest point, so it aims at the origin - the light `stc_main_light` caches at stage init. The other two light groups swap that for a cooler `(216, 216, 255)` ambient under a plain white key at `(-1000, 1000, 1500)`. A sphere-mapped gloss renders flatter in a viewer without sphere-map texgen than the game draws it.

Whatever the source, an image is re-encoded to suit the bank: RGB5A3 in a picture bank and I4 in a silhouette bank. Format is per frame and `HSD_TObjSetup` (`0x803f7158`) reads the CI-ness of the one frame it is drawing, so an RGB5A3 frame in a CMPR, C4 or C8 bank leaves every other frame alone and needs neither a CMPR encoder nor a quantizer. Its TLUT slot is filled with frame 4's and never consulted.

**How the splice runs.** A bank names its `TexAnim` by data-section offset, which is fixed for GKYE01, and the mod adds the loaded archive's `data` base to reach the live one. All ten archives load through `Gm_LoadGameFile` (`0x80059818`) and are rebuilt every time - the eight menu archives on each entry into their scene, freed by the shared menu teardown at `0x80131928` on the way out, and `IfAll2c.dat` / `IfAll3c.dat` on each City Trial and stadium 3D load - so the splice hangs off two hooks in that loader rather than running once. The first, at `0x80059834`, stashes the basename while it is still in `r25`, because the preload-hit path reuses that register as an allocation size before the tail. The second, at `0x800599f8` where those paths converge with the archive in `r30`, matches the name and patches. The engine passes the name with a trailing `.` and does not always match the disc's case (`MnSelplyCtAll` against `MnSelplyctAll.dat`, which `DVDConvertPathToEntrynum` accepts), so the match is case-insensitive and stops at the dot. The side-car and every machine's `.art` are held for the run of the game, because the banks are pointed straight at the images in them. Each load also records whether every bank in its archive grew, counting a preloaded archive whose image table is already the pool's, and the blip asks before it builds custom templates.

Three shapes of bank qualify, and the script has to tell them apart from the many other TexAnims in these archives. Most hold one image per character, so an image count of 20 identifies them. The picture banks hold one per character *color* and are wider than the roster, so they are matched on the shape the diverts leave on their ramp instead: a key on every frame `0` to `17`, none on King Dedede's kind 18 or Meta Knight's 19, and one on each of the frames they are diverted to. Matching only on frames 20 and 30 is not enough - `MnAll.dat`, `MnClCheckAll.dat` and `MnSelplym2dAll.dat` all key those frames for unrelated banks.

The small icon banks are the third shape and stop two short of the roster. Everything that draws one reaches the `CharacterKind` through `Machine_GetCKind` (`0x8000b9f4`), which never hands back King Dedede's kind or Meta Knight's - vanilla parks their machines elsewhere - so the banks hold 18 images, one per kind with a machine of its own, under a ramp keying frame `N` to image `N`. An identity ramp on its own fits any strip animation, so the count is what tells them from `MnClCheckAll.dat`'s 39 checklist tiles or `MnSelstadiumAll.dat`'s 24 stadium thumbnails. Their appended frames still start at 20, which leaves 18 and 19 unkeyed and holding image 17 exactly as vanilla does. Ungrown, an appended `CharacterKind` runs past the last key onto that same image, and every results row and the stadium select draw the Flight Warp Star.

The two HUD icon banks fit none of the three shapes - one image per machine kind, 18 and 17 of them - so the script names them by offset, checks that their ramp is the identity, and writes them with `key = 1`. For such a bank `ui_frames.c` fills appended frame *n* from the *n*-th registered machine rather than from `CharacterKind` `20 + n`, and it loads every registered machine's `.art` for that, whether or not the machine took a `CharacterKind`.

## A machine's own particle generators

A machine's animation bank names its trail by index into the vehicle particle bank, which the engine loads from `EfPtclVehicle.dat` as bank 0: a star's `vcAnimationStar` seven slots - two unknown slots at `+0x30`, two at `+0x38` for cruising and three at `+0x40` for boosting - and up to three joints at `+0x4c` to emit from, a bike's `vcAnimationWheel` one cruise slot at `+0x20`, one boost slot at `+0x24` and up to four joints at `+0x28`. The bank holds 52 generators. The first `0x3c` bytes of one are fixed fields - which of the bank's six texture groups to sample, size, fade rate, launch velocity, gravity, cone spread - and everything past that is bytecode, stepped once per frame per particle by `Ptcl_TickOne` (`0x8042cce8`). A byte below `0x80` is a wait of that many frames; `0x40 | wait` takes one more byte and sets the sprite image; `0xc0 | channels` and `0xd0 | channels` take a duration and then one byte per channel and ramp the particle's primary and secondary colors toward it; `0xff` ends.

So the color a particle wears is read out of the generator on the frame it spawns. `Ptcl_Alloc` (`0x8043294c`) reaches a generator as `descTable[bank][id]` from the table of per-bank descriptor arrays at `0x8058c708`, bounds-checked against the per-bank counts at `0x8058c608`; both are rebuilt on every 3D load.

The generator is bank data, not per-instance like `HSD_Material`, so a write to one of the bank's own generators reaches every machine emitting that index. A machine that wants a program to itself brings its own (`trail_bank.c`): up to four generator descriptors - header and program bytes, as a bank holds them on disc. `GetGenerator` hands a consumer the registry's copy of one, which is what the bank points at, so a write there reaches every particle that generator spawns afterward and nothing else on the field.

The bank's own generators are ids 0-51, and a machine's animation bank names its `k`-th own generator `52 + k`. Every registered machine can bring some, so each machine's are installed at a range of its own - the first machine's from 52, the next machine's past those - and discovery resolves each of its class's particle slots to the id it is installed at. The `Vehile_LoadFile` replacement writes those ids over every loaded copy of the machine's animation bank, whole rather than as an offset, so a copy that already took them is left as it is. The engine reads the slots live on every spawn, for both classes, and translates nothing.

The install is a hook at `0x802354bc`, the tail of `Ptcl_LoadEfPtclVehicle`, where every install path of that function meets with the descriptor table in place. `psInitDataBanks` (`0x8042a734`) has just pointed `psGeneratorDesc[PTCL_BANK_VEHICLE]` (bank 0) at the offset array inside the loaded archive and set `psGeneratorCount[PTCL_BANK_VEHICLE]` to its 52, so the hook copies those pointers into a mod-owned table sized for every registered machine's generators, where the machines' entries already sit at their ids, and repoints both. Nothing else writes either but the two particle teardowns (`0x8042ad44`, `0x8042af18`), which clear them, and both readers - `Ptcl_Alloc` and the spawn helper at `0x8042b350` - bound an id by the count alone and return 0 for a NULL entry, so the id of a generator discovery dropped emits nothing. An effect id reaches `Ptcl_Alloc` whole, with its bank taken as `id / 10000`, so the appended ids need nothing else. A vehicle bank holding more than 52 generators would have its own ids collide with the machines', so the table is then left as loaded and the machines emit nothing of their own.

It is redone on every load because the table is rebuilt each time, and it cannot be done lazily on the frame path: `Ptcl_Alloc` hands a generator node the descriptor's program pointer once, at creation, and the node holds it for as long as the machine's emitter lives. The entries point at the registry's own copies, taken at discovery because the archive is freed.

Nothing inside a descriptor is a pointer - loops store offsets from the program base, and textures and child generators are indexes - so the bytes need no relocation, with one exception: `psRelocDataBanks` (`0x8042a874`) rewrites the `PTCL_FLAGS_RELOC_MASK` bits (`0x0E000000`) of `PtclDesc.flags` in every descriptor it relocates to `PTCL_FLAGS_RELOCATED` (`0x08000000`), and a copy installed past that pass takes the same rewrite at boot. `scripts/hsd/ptcl.py` walks a program opcode by opcode, which is how `machine_descriptor.py` measures one, and its `color_offsets` lists where a program's color opcodes keep their RGB operands.

Cruise and boost want different generators. A cruise generator emits for as long as the machine is moving, so it is authored tight and short-lived; a boost generator is a wide, long-lived flare sized for the few seconds a boost lasts. The half-angle of the emission cone is the float at `+0x24` of the generator's fixed fields, in radians, and the emission rate is the float at `+0x28`. A boost generator in a cruise slot therefore reads as a permanent spray rather than a trail.

## The City Trial field spawn

`machine_spawn.c` owns the roll that decides which machine appears next on the City Trial field, because a registered machine has no way into the vanilla one. The engine reads a chance row out of `VcCommon.dat` (`(*stc_vcDataCommon)->spawn_data->spawn_desc[]`, three entries selected by match progress) with exactly `VCKIND_NUM` columns, and its selection loops are no wider - so an appended kind has neither a weight it could carry nor an index it could be picked at.

The two spawn sites are `CityMachineSpawn_DecideAndSpawn` (`0x801defac`, normal spawns) and `CityMachineSpawn_SpawnFormationStar` (`0x801df408`, the Machine Formation event). Both build a `u32` history-exclusion mask from `MachineSpawnData.prev_machine_kind[4]` and then roll in two passes, one summing the weights and one picking against `HSD_Randf`. The mask is built twice in separate registers, and the point between the two passes clobbers the `f1` holding the random result, so neither can be widened on its own and the whole selection is replaced - a hook at `0x801df00c` / `0x801df44c` skipping to `0x801df220` / `0x801df630`, with `r30` = `MachineSpawnData*` and `f1` = match progress on the way in and the chosen kind left in `r31` for the vanilla history write and `CityMachineSpawn_Create`.

The two rolls differ in width. The field roll's loop covers all 26 kinds, but Machine Formation's is 19 wide (`li r0, 19` at `0x801df4f8`, over a 4-byte stride), so the event only ever spawns stars: its replacement considers the vanilla star kinds and the registered stars, and the field replacement every kind. Both pick the chance row as vanilla does, the first entry whose threshold the match progress does not pass, and both keep vanilla's special case for a progress of exactly 1.0 - an `fcmpu` against the r2 constant 1.0 and a `beq` to row 0, at `0x801df07c` / `0x801df4d0` - which rolls the first window: `CityMachineSpawn_Think` pins the progress at 1.0 once the clock runs out.

The replacement seeds one weight per kind - the chance row for a vanilla kind, the descriptor's `spawn_weight` for a registered one - then keeps the two things the vanilla loop did that are worth keeping: the four-deep spawn history exclusion, shrunk to `min(spawnable - 1, 4)` so the only candidate cannot be excluded by its own history, and the weighted roll itself. A non-positive weight counts as 0, and if float rounding leaves the roll at the total, the last weighted kind is picked. With no consumer attached the result is the vanilla distribution plus whatever the descriptors ask for.

**Who gets weighed.** Every kind goes through a `CustomMachineSpawnWeightFilter` if a consumer registered one through `SetSpawnWeightFilter`, receiving the seeded weight and returning the one to use; 0 keeps a kind off the field. A consumer that gates machines returns 0 for a locked kind and can raise a kind the vanilla table gives 0 - the Compact Star, the Flight Warp Star and the two legendaries are all 0 in every window, so without that they could never appear whole on the field however they were unlocked. If a filter zeroes every kind there is nothing to roll, so the roll is skipped and the first kind the filter permits at an even weight of 1.0 - the first star, in Machine Formation - is spawned instead; that way a consumer gets one of its own back rather than a machine it refused, and one that permits nothing at all gets the Compact Star.

**Free Run.** Free Run is a separate placer. `CityMachineSpawn_PickFreeRunKind` (`0x801de41c`) keeps one machine of each of 18 kinds in fixed spots, drawing uniformly from the kinds whose count in `MachineSpawnData.freerun_placed` is 0, over a 0-25 loop that cannot reach an appended `MachineKind` - so it never places a custom machine, and has no spot to put one in.

`CityMachineSpawn_Init` seeds those counts one per starting player in every mode, and in Free Run `CityMachineSpawn_TakeMachine` (`0x801decbc`) and `CityMachineSpawn_ReleaseMachine` (`0x801ded8c`) - reached from `Machine_Destroy` and the Wheel and Bird abilities - move them, all keyed by `Machine_EncodeVehicleKind`, which answers a custom machine's own `MachineKind`. That kind indexes past the `VCKIND_NUM`-wide array and has no spot to be re-placed at, so it is kept out of the counts: a conditional hook on each helper's count (`0x801ded04`, `0x801dedd4`) skips past it for a kind at or above `VCKIND_NUM`, and a hook on `Init`'s increment (`0x801de2ec`) counts vanilla kinds only.

## The assembly cutscene

A machine whose descriptor names a cutscene archive can be put through the vanilla legendary assembly - the freeze, the scripted camera, the parts flying in on streaks of light, and the rider coming out mounted - with its own art. `machine_cinematic.c` owns it, and a consumer starts a run with `StartAssembly(kind, ply)`.

`VCKIND_DRAGOON` and `VCKIND_HYDRA` are accepted there too and fall through to the engine's own archives. Standing in at the seams below means this file already holds every condition a run has to satisfy, so a consumer handing out a whole legendary gets them for free rather than restating them; vanilla's own piece-collection path reaches `LegendaryMachine_StartAssembly` directly and is untouched either way.

Every machine-specific decision the engine's cutscene takes is reachable at a `bl`, so standing in at four is the whole of it:

| Site | Vanilla | Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| `0x80262be8`, the tail of `Preload_AllCityFiles` (`0x80262ba4`) | `LegendaryMachine_PreloadAssemblyArchives` queues `VsDragoon.dat` and `VsHydra.dat` for City Trial's ground | calls it, then, when the `GroundKind` it was handed is `GR_CITY1`, queues each registered machine's archive under the same `Preload_CreateEntry(5, path, 6, 6, 0, 1, 5, 0x20, 0)` |
| `0x80283914` in `LegendaryMachine_CreateAssembly` | `LegendaryMachine_LoadAssemblyArchive` picks `VsDragoon.dat` or `VsHydra.dat` off the machine index and returns its `vsData` | loads the machine's archive and returns its public, a block of the same shape |
| `0x80283c98`, in phase 3 of `LegendaryMachine_AssemblyThink` (`0x802839b8`) | `LegendaryMachine_FreeAssemblyArchive` | frees the machine's archive and clears the latch |
| `0x80283b70` in `LegendaryMachine_AssemblyThink` (`0x802839b8`) | `Ply_EnterLegendaryAssembly` poses the rider and stages the `(is_bike, class slot)` pair the substate's motion script feeds to `Rider_RespawnFullRecreate` 150 frames later | calls it, then overwrites that pair with the machine's own - either class - and the rider's `starting_machine_idx` with its kind, which is the whole of pointing the mount at a different machine |

A machine's archive has the shape `VsDragoon.dat` and `VsHydra.dat` have: one public, a `LegendaryAssemblyData` of three pointers - a glow-model triple `{JOBJDesc*, FigaTree*, MatAnimJoint*}`, the streaks the parts ride in on, which anchors the camera and whose FigaTree sets the run's 150-frame length; a parts-model triple of the same shape; and a pointer to a word holding the camera-animation descriptor. Everything the run draws comes out of that archive, so how it is authored is the machine's own affair. `cine_machine_index` picks which vanilla legendary the run borrows its rider pose, fanfare and sky preset from - nothing else downstream of the archive load reads it.

A run loads that archive synchronously mid-round through `lbLoadArchive` (`0x80059a20`), which panics (`__assert` -> `OSPanic`) on a public it cannot find, so the archive has to be both sound and resident before any run starts. `CustomMachineCinematic_OnBoot` loads each machine's archive once: `DVDConvertPathToEntrynum` confirms the file is on disc, `Archive_LoadFile` loads it inside `HSD_ArenaMark` / `HSD_ArenaRelease` so the boot arena takes it back, and `Archive_GetPublicAddress` looks for `cine_symbol`. A machine whose file or public is missing has its cutscene disabled there - `cine_machine_index` set to -1, and reported - and with no machine left holding one, none of the four calls is replaced. The preload row above is what makes a sound archive resident; City Trial is the only ground the vanilla call queues for, and the only scene a run is allowed in.

One cutscene runs at a time, which is the engine's own limit: `GameData.legendary_assembly_gobj` holds a single controller GObj and a second run would tear down the first one's models. A latch names whichever registered machine started the run, and the vanilla pair plays untouched while it is clear. `On3DLoadStart` clears it, because a scene change tears the cutscene's GObjs down without reaching its own last phase.

`StartAssembly` returns 0, and what the player gets instead is the caller's decision, when any of these does not hold:

- **The scene is City Trial, and not the title demo.** The cutscene stages its models on the open CT map and drives that scene's sky and area lights; a stadium or an Air Ride race dereferences a null jobj or trips the area-light assert. The attract demo runs a real City Trial round inside `MJRKIND_TITLE`, where a cutscene would take over the title loop, so `Gm_IsAutoDemo()` rejects it too.
- **The rider is Kirby.** `Rider_EnterLegendaryAssembly` (`0x8019248c`) is Kirby-only and the mount rides on the state it enters, so anyone else would get the whole shot and no machine. They take the plain mount instead.
- **The kind has a cutscene.** A registered machine whose descriptor names none, or whose archive failed the boot check, is refused, as is every vanilla kind but the two legendaries.
- **The player is in range and has both a machine and a rider.**
- **Nothing is already running**, by the latch or by `Gm_IsLegendaryAssembling` (`0x8000c934`).
- **The same cutscene has not already run this scene.** A registered machine's archive takes exactly the vanilla path - queued by the same `Preload_CreateEntry` call, loaded by the same `lbLoadArchive` and freed by the same `Archive_Free(0, arc)` when the run ends - and `Preload_GetArchiveByFilename` keeps handing back the freed block, so a second run in the same scene loads a joint out of freed memory and crashes in `JObj_LoadJoint`. A second latch, cleared with the others at `On3DLoadStart`, holds one bit per cutscene: one per vanilla legendary and one per registered machine.

## The plain mount

`MountMachine(kind, ply)` puts a player straight onto a machine with no presentation, through `Rider_RespawnFullRecreate` (`0x80193900`) - the recreate the cutscene's motion script ends in - and sets the rider's `starting_machine_idx` to the kind so a later respawn keeps it. It is what a player gets when the cutscene cannot run for them, and it takes any kind under the ceiling, vanilla kinds included.

`machine_mount.c` queues the kind per player and runs the recreate from the mod's `OnFrameStart`. The recreate tears down the rider's current machine, and a caller inside `Machine_OnTouchItem` or a collision callback is still running on it, so it cannot happen where the caller is. A player with no rider is refused at the call, and a mount still queued when a scene loads is dropped at `On3DLoadStart`.

## Consuming the registry

`Hoshi_ImportMod(CUSTOM_MACHINES_MOD_NAME, ...)` returns a `CustomMachinesAPI`. Import it after `OnBoot`: hoshi installs mods in `/mods` FST order, which is alphabetical, so most consumers boot before this one and an `OnBoot` import answers NULL. Treat NULL as "no custom machine exists" and fall back to the vanilla ceilings - `custom_machines_api.h` publishes four inlines that do exactly that (`CustomMachines_KindNum`, `CustomMachines_CharacterKindNum`, `CustomMachines_ResolveKind`, `CustomMachines_ClassIndexOf`), each taking the imported pointer and answering with hoshi's own value when it is NULL.

That header documents every call. The ones carrying a design decision rather than a lookup:

- `FindKindByName(name)` is the only handle that ties a specific drop-in `.dat` to feature code; a display name is the link, since kinds are assigned in scan order and nothing may hardcode one. Discovery refuses a second machine under a name already taken, so the answer is never ambiguous.
- `SetAvailabilityFilter` and `SetSpawnWeightFilter` are how a consumer gates a machine. Each replaces one answer inside machinery this mod owns - the select screens' packing and the City Trial spawn roll - rather than the machinery itself, and each is asked per rebuild and per spawn, so a filter may be set from any scene. One mod owns that policy, so each holds a single filter.
- `SetInitHandler` / `SetThinkHandler` / `SetAnimHandler` claim a registered machine's per-kind slots for per-machine work a consumer has no hook of its own for: Init and Think on the engine's own tables for the star class and on the Wheel class's epilogues for a bike, Anim on `Machine_AnimThink`'s last call for both. This mod implements no machine's behavior itself; a visual effect such as a color cycle runs from Anim, after the ColAnim overlays, and reaches what it writes through `GetMachineJoint` and `GetGenerator`. `GetMachineJoint` resolves a depth-first index against the archive's `JObjDesc` tree and finds the live joint through the back-pointer `JObjLoad` (`0x8040add4`) leaves at `JOBJ+0x84`, so it does not depend on how the engine roots the instance.
- `AddDeathHandler` is how a consumer sees KOs. This mod replaces the engine's only `bl Ply_AddDeath`, in `Machine_GiveDamage`, to count them per widened kind, so a second mod cannot replace the same call; it tells up to four handlers instead, after the engine's own bookkeeping has run, with the victim, the victim's `DmgLog` and the widened kind that was being ridden.
- `StartAssembly(kind, ply)` runs the legendary assembly cutscene on a registered machine, `VCKIND_DRAGOON` and `VCKIND_HYDRA` included, and `MountMachine(kind, ply)` is the plain mount for when it cannot.

The API is exported even when the FST folder holds no machine at all, because this mod owns the select screens' packing either way and a consumer still needs the filter.

Two mods import it. `ap_star` owns the Archipelago Star's own behavior and resolves the machine by name to reach it. `archipelago` requires it: every ceiling it reads comes from here, its gating is the two filters - the select-screen availability filter and the spawn weight filter - plus the legendary hand-out, which goes through `StartAssembly` rather than driving the cutscene itself, and its Destruction Derby check reads KOs through a death handler. It gates the Archipelago Star, found by name, alongside the vanilla machines, and leaves every other registered machine ungated.

## Known gaps

These are the places a custom machine is still second-class.

- **The registry holds 13 machines.** The select screens carry 33 icons, 20 of them the vanilla roster, and `CUSTOM_MACHINE_MAX` is sized to that so every registered machine that wants a character gets one. A 14th archive in `machines/` is skipped, and the scan reports it.

- **A long select list overruns LAN controller-claim state.** A list of more than 22 icons on Air Ride or 21 on City Trial runs onto the per-slot controller-claim arrays that follow it. Offline nothing touches those arrays past their initialisation, which runs before the list is built, so only multi-console (LAN) play with that many icons on screen loses its controller claiming.

- **A machine borrows a vanilla voice where it ships no sound.** Its `audio_kind` supplies every audio row role its `.ssm` leaves empty, the script envelope each drop-in sound plays through, and its class sound table entry, which no `.ssm` role reaches. The assembly cutscene likewise runs under a vanilla legendary's rider pose, fanfare and sky.
